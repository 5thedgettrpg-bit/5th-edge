Why We Rebuilt the Wizard

Fixing the 5e Wizard without solving the problem by simply giving it more spell power.

Published October 6, 2026 · Class Design

The Wizard has never lacked power. What it has often lacked is a satisfying class chassis when the campaign does not give the spellbook time to become a campaign-long project. Our goal was to raise the Wizard's floor, sharpen its identity, and make staying Wizard feel rewarding without simply handing it bigger spell slots or higher save DCs.

The 2014 problem

The 2014 Wizard has an extraordinary ceiling. Given time, gold, scrolls, and knowledge of the adventure ahead, it can solve a huge range of problems. The tradeoff is that much of what makes the class feel special lives outside the class table. The spell list does most of the work while the base class has long stretches where the reward is primarily access to higher-level spells.

That structure is especially noticeable in one-shots. You may never copy a spell, never benefit from long-term research, and never experience the spellbook as a growing library. In that format the Wizard can feel less like a scholar of magic and more like a full caster whose best feature is simply having access to good spells.

What the 2024 revision improves

The 2024 rules give the Wizard better scaffolding and reinforce scholarly play. We like that direction. For 5th Edge, though, we wanted to go further and make the spellbook itself the engine of the class rather than mostly a container for prepared magic.

The spellbook becomes the class engine

In 5th Edge, the spellbook is the Wizard's arcane focus, preparation library, ritual reference, and gateway to learning magic found in the world. The Wizard prepares a number of spells of each spell level equal to its Intelligence modifier, with a minimum of one, and can swap a number of prepared spells equal to its Intelligence modifier after a short or long rest.

Spell scrolls matter too. Copying a scroll requires an Intelligence (Arcana) check against DC 10 + the spell's level, and the scroll is consumed whether the check succeeds or fails. A Wizard can even learn a spell from another class list if the spell's school matches the school associated with that Wizard's Arcane Tradition. Once learned, it becomes a Wizard spell for that character.

Raise the floor, not the spell ceiling

We added class features that matter even if the Wizard never finds a scroll. Reactive Casting turns an opportunity attack into a single-target spell with a one-action casting time. Enhanced Concentration grants advantage on concentration saves caused by damage. Scholar makes Arcana checks dependable. Signature Casting gives the Wizard a signature attack cantrip and later extends Intelligence-based damage to Spell Mastery and Signature Spells.

Those features absolutely add power, but they do not increase the class's spell-slot progression, save DC, or access to higher-level spells. The goal is a class that feels better every session, not a class that simply pushes the existing full-caster ceiling higher.

The high-level arc

Advanced Ritualist reduces the extra ritual casting time from ten minutes to one. Memorized Spells lets the Wizard internalize a number of spells equal to its Intelligence modifier, preserving those spells even if the book is lost. Spell Mastery turns selected 1st- and 2nd-level spells into at-will magic, and Signature Spells grants limited free casting of two 3rd-level spells.

First you rely on the book. Then you master the book. Eventually, some of the book lives in you.

Is it stronger?

Probably, especially in consistency. But the 2014 Wizard already has one of the highest theoretical ceilings in the game. Our revision spends more of its power budget on reliability, identity, flexibility, and reasons to remain single-class.

The real test is whether Sorcerer, Warlock, Cleric, Bard, and Druid still have compelling things they do better. That is the standard we will use as the rest of the caster redesigns move forward.
