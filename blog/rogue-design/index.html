Why We Rebuilt the Rogue

Keeping the Rogue's best ideas while making the whole class—not just Sneak Attack—feel essential.

Published October 6, 2026 · Class Design

The Rogue fantasy is not just “the character with Sneak Attack.” It is the character who is hard to catch unprepared, dangerous with modest weapons, and reliable when everyone else is gambling on the d20. Our revision pushes the class toward that identity from level 1 to 20.

The 2014 foundation is good, but narrow

The 2014 Rogue has one of the clearest class loops in fifth edition: expertise, Cunning Action, Sneak Attack, defensive reactions, and Reliable Talent. The weakness is that much of the class's combat progression funnels through one damage feature. When Sneak Attack lands, the class feels correct. When the situation suppresses it, a large part of the class can disappear.

What we kept

Cunning Action, Uncanny Dodge, Evasion, Reliable Talent, Blindsense, Slippery Mind, and Stroke of Luck remain recognizable pieces of the Rogue. We were not interested in discarding the parts that already work.

What changes the feel

At 1st level, Life of Crime adds half proficiency to ability checks that do not already include proficiency and retains Thieves' Cant. Rogue Weapon Training lets light or finesse weapons use at least a d8 damage die. Together those features make the Rogue feel broadly capable before Sneak Attack even enters the picture.

Sneak Attack begins at 2nd level and uses d8s in the 5th Edge progression. It scales less often, but each increase is more meaningful. That keeps Sneak Attack important without making every single level of Rogue balance revolve around one ever-growing pile of d6s.

Follow Up rewards finishing the job

At 5th level, Follow Up gives the Rogue something to do when an attack drops a hostile creature to 0 hit points: either reposition without provoking opportunity attacks or make a thrown-weapon attack against another target in normal range. It reinforces momentum and mobility instead of simply adding another static damage bonus.

Reliability becomes the late-game identity

Reliable Talent arrives at 9th level. Deadly Precision adds proficiency bonus to Sneak Attack damage at 14th. At 20th, Reliable Strikes allows a finesse or ranged weapon attack roll of 9 or lower to be treated as a 10, while a natural 1 still misses.

The Rogue's endpoint is not “roll more dice.” It is “I am incredibly difficult to make fail at the things I built this character to do.”

Subclass cadence matters too

Roguish Archetype begins at 1st level, with features at 1, 3, 7, 11, and 17. That lets the Rogue's profession and fantasy show up immediately instead of feeling like a generic pre-subclass chassis for the opening session.

Compared with 2024

The 2024 Rogue improves the class's tactical expression and gives Sneak Attack more ways to matter. We share the goal of making the Rogue more than a repetitive damage packet, but 5th Edge leans harder into universal competence, weapon reliability, and subclass identity from level 1.
